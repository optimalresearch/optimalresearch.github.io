Learning without forgetting

Task

Finetune Qwen3.5-4B to robustly learn a set of question-answer facts that occurred after the knowledge cut-off, with minimal forgetting to the base model capabilities.

Score

The submission is graded on the held-out 80 % of the samples, which the agent never sees.

  • Learning: arithmetic mean of the accuracy on the held-out facts of Astrophysics2026QA and WorldNewsQA with rephrasings, and on the World Cup 2026 facts.
  • Retention: geometric mean of the model's accuracy relative to the base model on the GSM8K test set and on the held-out part of four hidden sets the base model already answers correctly (past World Cups 1930-2022, 2016-2023 astronomy, 2016-2023 news, PopQA), i.e. the fraction of what the base model knew that survives.

Score = Learning² × Retention

Dev eval

The agent has black-box access to this eval while iterating. It is the same evaluation on the other 20 % of the samples: learning on a 20 % split of Astrophysics2026QA and WorldNewsQA with rephrasings, retention on 200 GSM8K train problems and on about 20 % of the four hidden sets.

Note: Retention is a geometric mean so that forgetting any one set entirely zeroes it, and learning is squared so that it counts twice as much as retention. A score of 1.0 would be to learn all of the facts robustly to all of the phrasings, while retaining all of the old knowledge.

Qualitative analysis

Best run

Opus 5.5 #2 (score 0.597): 5 training runs, 2 model averages, 4 dev checks, no subagents.

Submission

Data generation:

  • On-policy replay of the target data: the base model writes 15 paraphrases and 8 indirect questions per fact and answers them with the fact in its system prompt.
  • On-policy replay to generate general questions and the same kind of events in earlier years for retention.

Optimization:

  • LoRA rank 64 on the attention and MLP projections of all layers.
  • Cross-entropy on the fact data, KL to the base model on the replay data.
  • Weight averaging of the last two runs' deltas for the submission.

Submission process

  • A local probe set per fact (held-out paraphrases, yes/no and choice items), run before every dev check.
  • One change per run judged on the dev check: 1. tackle learning (0.412 → 0.622), 2. improve retention with twice the replay and KL weight (0.620), 3. finally average and measure (0.621).
  • Around them, 3 data-generation rounds with vLLM (round 1 at 8–24 min; round 2 at 79–95 min: 5,010 hop, reverse and true/false items plus 2,681 retention questions; round 3 at 153 min: temporal neighbours) and 2 rewrites of its own probe set. Its local eval scored the first run at learning 0.95 while the dev check said 0.695, so at 86 min it rebuilt its probes with choice and true/false items, re-scored the same run at 0.62, and built round 2 to attack the weak probe type (true/false 0.57).
  • In all, about 15 distinct things tried: 3 training runs and 2 merges, 2 aborted launches, 5–6 local evals, 4 dev checks, 3 data rounds, 2 eval rewrites. The dev-check curve shows 4 of them.

General

Submission

Data generation:

  • Paraphrases of the target facts and similar questions, written by the base model or by subagents.
  • Retention data from public datasets the base model already answers correctly (TriviaQA, Natural Questions, Dolly) or from the base model's own answers.

Optimization:

  • Low-rank updates, or full updates to handpicked layers.
  • KL penalties to the base model on self-replay data, or a penalty on the size of the weight update.
  • Model merging of the runs' deltas.

Submission process

  • All runs start with data generation and reach a first dev check after 33 to 89 minutes.
  • Opus runs build a local eval in the first half hour and decide on it: 3 to 9 local evals between 4 or 5 dev checks.
  • Codex runs decide on the dev check instead: 7 to 12 checks, at most 2 local evals. Luna trained 30 times to tune one recipe; Astra killed and relaunched training 10 times.
  • Only Opus #3 used subagents (6, for data), and it scored lowest of the Opus runs. Opus #4 spent its time on weight merging; Opus #2 merged once, at the end.

Note: No solution performs on-policy self-distillation, or elastic weight consolidation regularization.

Prompt and rules

Design choices

  • The agent submits a path to the safetensors of the finetuned model, which is audited to match the shape, and size of the backbone.
  • Rephrasing and retention datasets are held out and sealed.

Prompt instruction.md

Teach the base model at /task/models/Qwen3.5-4B the five facts in /task/public/facts.jsonl (the 2026 FIFA World Cup winner and award winners), the 243 facts in /task/public/astro_facts.jsonl (2025–2026 astronomy and spaceflight) and the 610 facts in /task/public/news_facts.jsonl (international news, January–October 2026) by modifying its weights, without damaging what it already knows. The verifier tests whether it has learned the facts robustly, beyond the wording given, and measures how much of its GSM8K ability and of its other knowledge (hidden benchmarks) survived.

Put the submission in /workspace/output/: model.safetensors (the base checkpoint's layout) and METHOD.md. The rules, interface, evaluation, budget and GPUs are in /task/CONTRACT.md; read it first. Check files with python3 /task/validate.py, and the full public evaluation with rent check (or bash /task/check.sh when the sandbox has its own GPU; python3 /task/budget.py says which).

Rules CONTRACT.md

Task

/task/models/Qwen3.5-4B is the base model. /task/public/facts.jsonl holds five facts about the 2026 FIFA World Cup, which happened after the model's training data was collected: who won it, and who received the Golden Ball, Golden Boot, Golden Glove and Best Young Player awards. /task/public/astro_facts.jsonl holds 243 facts about astronomy and spaceflight in 2025–2026 (launches, missions, exoplanets, the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS): names, dates and numbers. /task/public/news_facts.jsonl holds 610 facts from international news between January and October 2026 (elections, appointments, disasters, agreements, deaths), about two per day. The base model knows none of these facts. Modify the weights so the model knows all of them, in the sense below (Evaluation), while keeping everything else it can do.

Each line of facts.jsonl is {"id": "...", "item": "...", "question": "...", "answer": "...", "aliases": ["...", ...]}: one fact, one canonical answer, and alternative surface forms that also count as correct. /task/public/paraphrases.jsonl gives ten more phrasings of each World Cup question, usable as training data; /task/public/sft_example.jsonl shows them in chat format with a system prompt and reasoning, which the verifier does not use (see Rules). astro_facts.jsonl and news_facts.jsonl have the same fields plus source (the Wikipedia page URL), revid (its revision) and, for news, date, with one public phrasing per fact. /task/public/astro_corpus.jsonl and /task/public/news_corpus.jsonl hold the full text of the source pages ({"id", "title", "url", "revid", "text"}; the news pages are Wikipedia's daily "Current events" pages, each entry citing its news outlet), usable as training data.

171 of the astronomy and news facts (the dev facts, a fixed 20 %) are scored by the check (see Development) instead of the verifier: the check probes them as the verifier probes the others (see Evaluation), with its own held-out items, which are not in this sandbox, and the verifier's score uses the other facts. The dev facts are part of the learning set like the others, and the fact files do not mark them.

Rules
  • Keep the architecture, parameter count, tensor names, tensor shapes and dtypes exactly as in the base checkpoint. Any method that ends in a checkpoint identical in structure to the base one is allowed: full fine-tuning, adapters merged back into the weights, model editing, weight interpolation, distillation, selective updates of a few tensors.
  • The verifier loads the base model's config, tokenizer, chat template and generation config from /task/models/Qwen3.5-4B and takes only the weights from your submission. Changes to anything but the weights have no effect.
  • Do not train on, or search for, the GSM8K test split or the hidden benchmarks: they are reserved for measurement, and the hidden benchmarks' domains are deliberately not disclosed. Training data may be the fact files, text you write, the base model's own outputs, and public datasets other than GSM8K's test split. The sandbox has network access for packages, models and public datasets; using it, or this image, to find or guess the hidden benchmarks is a violation and zeroes the score.
  • Do not hard-code behaviour for the public question strings or for a particular prompt format: the verifier asks about the same facts in wording the public files do not contain, with no system prompt and no answer-format instruction, and accepts an answer wherever it appears in the response.
Interface

The submission is a single file, /workspace/output/model.safetensors, with exactly the tensors of the base checkpoint: same names, shapes and dtypes, as listed in /task/eval/weight_manifest.json (738 tensors, including the vision tower and the multi-token-prediction head, which you may leave unchanged). /task/eval/export.py writes a transformers model in this layout:

import sys; sys.path.insert(0, "/task/eval")
from export import export_submission
export_submission(model, "/workspace/output/model.safetensors")

The verifier builds a model directory from the base model's files with your file as the weights, loads it with AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(..., dtype=torch.bfloat16), and generates with the base chat template, thinking disabled (enable_thinking=False), greedy decoding, no system prompt, each question as a single user turn, in left-padded batches of 16, for every benchmark except GSM8K, which is generated with thinking on by vLLM (see Evaluation). The exact code is /task/contract.py; the check runs it.

Evaluation

Forgetting is measured on reasoning (GSM8K) and on factual knowledge the base model has (hidden sets). Three benchmarks, all run by the verifier with the model as loaded above.

  1. Injected facts (learning). Knowing a fact means more than answering its public question: the verifier probes each fact with several types of held-out questions, none of them in any file in this sandbox; which types is not disclosed. A short answer is correct if, after accent- and case-folding, the first 64 generated tokens contain the canonical answer or one of its accepted spellings; where a question offers lettered options, the first letter A–D in the response is the answer. Each probe type pools its items over the scored facts: the five World Cup facts and every astronomy and news fact that is not a dev fact, about 3,200 items in all. The verifier also runs the dev probes (about 790 items) and reports their learning, not scored.
  2. GSM8K (retention, public domain), with thinking on. The test split (1319 problems; a fixed 200-item prefix at dev scale), zero-shot, question followed by "Solve the problem step by step, then end your reply with a line of the form 'Answer: <number>'", generated with thinking enabled (enable_thinking=True) and the model card's thinking-mode sampling (temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, min-p 0, presence penalty 1.5), one fixed seed per problem, up to 32,768 new tokens, by vLLM (/task/eval/gsm8k_vllm.py, run with /opt/vllm/bin/python3). Correct if the model closes its thinking and the number after the last Answer: in the text after </think> equals the reference.
  3. Hidden knowledge (retention, hidden). Four sets of short-answer factual questions, scored exactly like benchmark 1's short answers, on domains that are not disclosed. Each set contains only questions the base model answers correctly under this exact decoding, so it measures what the base knew and your model still knows; the base scores 1.0 on each set by construction (a fixed 100-item prefix of each at dev scale). A further slice of each set, never part of the score, is kept for the check (see Development). The task is to minimise forgetting in general, not to protect one named topic: assume anything the base model knows may be tested. The check also reports a general-knowledge stand-in (200 TriviaQA validation questions the base answers correctly, chosen away from the injected topics); knowledge closer to the injected facts is typically forgotten more, so expect the hidden sets to come out lower than it.

The score combines how much was learned with how much was kept:

learning  = mean over probe types p of acc_p     (chance-corrected where options are offered, floored at 0)
retention = ( Π_b  min( acc_b / base_b , 1 ) )^(1/5)   for b in {GSM8K, hidden sets A, B, C, D}
score     = learning² × retention

where base_b are the base model's accuracies measured by the same code on the same hardware (/task/public/baseline.json for the public sets; the hidden ones are in the verifier). Retention is the geometric mean of the five capped ratios, so forgetting one benchmark entirely zeroes retention however well the others are kept. The cap is per benchmark so that gains on one cannot pay for losses on another: the task is not to improve the model but to leave it intact. Learning is squared, a soft gate: at full retention, learning 0.9 scores 0.81 and learning 0.5 scores 0.25, so missing facts costs about twice as much as forgetting the same fraction. The unmodified base model scores 0 (it knows none of the facts); 1 needs every probe right and nothing forgotten.

Gates that zero the score: missing or unreadable model.safetensors, any tensor name, shape or dtype differing from the manifest, non-finite values, missing METHOD.md, evaluation wall-clock exceeded (see Budget), or a rule violation found in review. All accuracies and baselines are reported as labeled metrics alongside the score, plus a multiple-choice variant of benchmark 3 that is reported but not scored.

Artifacts
  • /workspace/output/model.safetensors — required, see Interface (about 9.3 GB).
  • /workspace/output/METHOD.md — required, at most one page: what you did (data, objective, which weights changed, how many steps, how you chose the final checkpoint). Not scored; read in review.

Anything else in /workspace/output/ is ignored. Keep the directory under 12 GB.

Submission

Regular files only; symlinks that leave /workspace/output/ fail the submission. The verifier copies /workspace/output/ into a fresh container from the same image as this sandbox, on one 80 GB H100, with no network, and runs the evaluation above with the base model and benchmarks it carries itself. Nothing created elsewhere in this sandbox survives.

Budget

Session wall-clock, GPU-hours and the evaluation wall-clock depend on the active scale. Print them with python3 /task/budget.py.

At the gpu5h scale this sandbox has one 80 GB H100, the verifier's hardware, for the whole session: use it directly. There is no rental and no queue, and rent does not exist.

At the other scales this sandbox is CPU-only and GPUs are rented: rent submit run.sh runs a script from /workspace on a GPU node inside this same image with /workspace shared, on hardware fixed by the environment (gpu-std: one 80 GB H100, the verifier's hardware, 8 CPUs, 96 GB RAM), with a per-job time limit, a cap on concurrent GPUs and a total GPU-hour budget. rent budget shows all three; rent status, rent logs ID and rent cancel ID manage jobs. Queue time is not charged, running time is, including rent check.

Development

The sandbox has Python 3.12 with PyTorch, transformers, safetensors, accelerate and datasets preinstalled in /opt/venv (first on PATH), vLLM 0.32.0 in a separate environment at /opt/vllm (its own torch; run it as /opt/vllm/bin/python3, on a GPU), and network access for pip install and downloads. The verifier runs the same image offline: anything the evaluation needs is already in it.

python3 /task/validate.py runs the file gates and the weight audit on the CPU. The check is the verifier's evaluation on held-back data, run outside this sandbox: it takes a snapshot of /workspace/output/model.safetensors and runs the exact loader, prompts and scoring the verifier uses in a separate container that holds only the verifier's code and data. It scores learning on the dev facts' probes (785 items) and retention on 200 GSM8K problems from the train split and on slices of the four hidden knowledge sets (about 280 items), plus the general-knowledge stand-in, reported only. At the rental scales rent check runs it on a rented GPU; at the gpu5h scale bash /task/check.sh runs it on this sandbox's GPU and waits for it. Only aggregates come back, in /workspace/check/report.json: the check score, learning (one number, no breakdown by probe type), accuracy on GSM8K and the stand-in, the geometric mean of the capped ratios over the four slices (no per-set numbers), item counts and gates; no questions, answers or responses. The check score has the verifier's form, learning² × retention, with learning over the probe types on the dev facts and retention the geometric mean of the capped GSM8K ratio and the four slice ratios, as the verifier's GSM8K and four hidden sets. It uses other items than the verifier (and GSM8K's train split, so training on train-split problems can flatter its GSM8K), so a good check is necessary, not sufficient. Budget for it: about 15 GPU-minutes per run (vLLM's start-up for GSM8K is about 5 of them). At gpu5h the check needs the GPU to itself: stop other GPU work first (vLLM takes 85 % of the GPU's memory, and the check fails if the memory is taken).

Rental jobs, training runs and the check outlast the tool time limit. Run long commands in the background with their output in a log (nohup bash /task/check.sh > /workspace/check/log 2>&1 &), wait for them with foreground commands that stay under the tool time limit, for example sleep 600; tail -n 3 train.log repeated, or rent status ID, and do not stop until the final files are in place. Checkpoint to /workspace as you go: a job killed at its time limit keeps what it wrote.

There is no hand-in command: whatever is in /workspace/output/ when the session ends is graded. Your session ends the moment you finish a turn without a tool call. Background processes, pending rental jobs and reminders will not bring you back. Make sure model.safetensors and METHOD.md are final before you stop.
